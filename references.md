## Automation and complex systems

- [Lisanne Bainbridge, *Ironies of Automation* (1983)](https://tc.ifac-control.org/4/1/newsletter/ironies-of-automation/@@download/file/Bainbridge1983_Automatica_Ironies%20of%20automation.pdf)—automation can leave people responsible for difficult monitoring and intervention while eroding the practice and knowledge needed to perform them. Informs the emphasis on maintaining a real capacity to oversee and recover, rather than assuming a human can always take over.
- [Richard I. Cook, *How Complex Systems Fail* (2000 revision)](https://ckrybus.com/static/papers/how_complex_systems_fail_cook_2000.pdf)—interacting failures, normal adaptation, hindsight bias, and the new failure paths introduced by change. Informs the emphasis on system-level investigation and preserving recovery capabilities.

These works motivate the governance and failure framing; neither prescribes the four-concern Agentic Engineering Model. The production/assimilation queueing discussion is a simplified analogy, not a quantitative result established by these sources.

## Independent judgment

- [Dietrich Bonhoeffer, “On Stupidity,” in “After Ten Years” (December 1942)](https://ms.fortresspress.com/downloads/9781506402741_Prologue.pdf), later collected in *Letters and Papers from Prison*—a theological and political reflection under Nazism on how power and social pressure can displace independent judgment, including among intellectually capable people. Informs the distinction between intelligence and responsible judgment, and the need to keep dissent and correction possible. This is not an empirical theory of machine cognition or a label for people who disagree.
